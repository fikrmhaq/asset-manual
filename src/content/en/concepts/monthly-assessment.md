Every asset unit belonging to a department should have **at least one assessment
recorded during each month**. An assessment is simply a
[history entry](/concepts/history) somebody wrote after looking at the item — its
condition, where it is, and a note saying what they found.

The point is not paperwork. A registry nobody checks drifts quietly: items move,
break, or disappear, and the record still says what it said a year ago. A monthly
check is what keeps the register describing reality.

## What counts as an assessment

Exactly one thing: a history entry recorded by a person, through **Record a
change** on the unit.

What does **not** count:

- Entries the application wrote itself when a unit was **borrowed or returned**.
  Lending an item out is not an inspection of it. If borrowing counted, a
  department could satisfy a month by lending its own equipment.
- Registering the unit. Creating a record is not looking at the object.

> [!NOTE]
> A return can carry a note somebody typed, and that note is still shown on the
> unit's timeline. It simply does not discharge the monthly obligation.

## Who it applies to

The department's **person in charge**. Each department has one, and their
assessments are what count towards that department's coverage.

An institution administrator can record an assessment on any unit, and it is a
perfectly real entry on the timeline — but it does not count towards the
department's coverage, because the obligation belongs to the department. The
Dashboard reports those separately so a shortfall is explainable rather than
looking like nothing happened.

A department with **nobody assigned** is reported as such rather than as 0%. It
has not failed to check its units; it has nobody to ask.

## Coverage counts units, not entries

> **Coverage = distinct units assessed ÷ units the department is responsible for**

Assessing the same unit three times is **one** unit covered. Two people checking
the same item is still one. This is the single most important thing to understand
about the figure: it measures the estate, not the effort.

Effort is measured separately, on the **Assessment activity** card, which counts
entries *and* distinct units in two different columns for exactly this reason.

## Which units are counted

A unit counts towards its department's total when it is active, is not disposed
of or deactivated, and was registered before the month ended.

**Units out on loan still count.** The department remains responsible for them,
the borrower can be contacted, and excluding them would let a department improve
its figure by lending things out. Units in for maintenance count too — a unit
being repaired is precisely one whose condition someone should be recording.

Units with **no department** count towards nobody. They appear separately on the
Dashboard, and the fix for them is to assign a department.

## Which month an assessment lands in

The month it was **written**, not the date it describes.

You can backdate the *effective date* of an entry — recording on 2 August that a
unit has been damaged since 31 July is correct and useful, and the timeline will
show it applying from July. But it counts towards **August's** coverage, because
that is when the work happened. A closed month cannot be improved after the fact.

Months run on the institution's local calendar (Asia/Jakarta), so an assessment
recorded at 6am on the 1st belongs to the new month, as you would expect.

## Nothing is blocked

This is measured and shown, never enforced. Outstanding assessments do not stop
anyone signing in, editing a unit, or doing anything else. The Dashboard tells
you where you stand; acting on it is a management matter, not a software one.
