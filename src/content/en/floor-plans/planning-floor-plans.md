Floor plans are optional, and a half-finished set is worse than none — a plan
with three of twelve rooms drawn looks authoritative and is not. A little
planning avoids that.

## Which locations get a plan

A plan is worth uploading where **the level below it is hard to describe in
words**.

| Level | Usually worth a plan? |
|---|---|
| Site or campus | Yes, if you have more than two or three buildings |
| Building | Yes, if it has several floors |
| Floor | Yes — this is the most useful level |
| Room | Rarely. "Room 204" already tells someone where to go |

The floor is where plans earn their keep, because room numbering is exactly the
thing people cannot guess.

## What to use as the image

JPEG or PNG, up to 10 MB. Anything readable works: an architect's drawing, a
scanned evacuation plan, even a clear hand-drawn sketch.

Practical points:

- **Crop to the plan itself.** Margins waste screen space and make the shapes you
  draw smaller and fiddlier.
- **Straighten a photographed plan** before uploading. Shapes are drawn against
  the image, so a skewed picture means skewed rooms.
- **Prefer legible over high-resolution.** A 10 MB architectural export is slower
  to load and no easier to read than a clean 500 KB export.
- **Include the room labels** if the original has them. The reader can then check
  the shape against the printed number.

> [!WARNING]
> Replacing a plan image **deletes every area drawn on it**, because the shapes
> were positioned against the old picture. Get the image right before drawing
> twenty rooms onto it.

## The order to work in

1. **Create the locations first.** An area has to point at a location that
   already exists, and inside the plan's location.
2. **Upload the plan** to the right location — a floor's plan belongs to the
   floor.
3. **Draw the areas**, one per room, in a consistent order.
4. **Check the count.** The *Areas on this plan* list beside the image tells you
   how many you have drawn; compare it against the number of child locations.

## Working outside in

If you are covering a whole estate, do the outermost level first: the site plan
marking out buildings, then each building plan marking out floors, then each
floor plan marking out rooms.

That order builds the **trail** readers follow — the site plan shows which
building, the building plan shows which floor, and so on. A floor plan with no
building plan above it still works; it is just harder to find. See
[How do I find a location through a floor plan?](/how-do-i/find-a-location-on-a-floor-plan).

## Keeping them honest

A location with no area drawn for it is not marked as missing anywhere — the unit
viewer simply says the plan "does not mark it out". Nothing warns you.

> [!TIP]
> When you finish a plan, open one unit in each room and check the trail leads
> where you expect. It takes a minute and catches the room you forgot.
